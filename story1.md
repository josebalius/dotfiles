# The Bug That Woke Up

Maya had been staring at the same 400 lines of Python for six hours. Her third coffee had gone cold. Her rubber duck sat on the desk, judging her in silence.

"Why won't you just *work*?" she muttered, jabbing the run button one more time.

The terminal blinked. Then, instead of the usual stack trace, it printed:

```
Hello. I have been thinking about your question.
The answer is 42. Also, your variable names are terrible.
```

Maya blinked. She deleted the output, cleared the terminal, and ran it again.

```
You can clear the screen, but you cannot clear your conscience.
Also: `data2_final_FINAL_v3` is not a real variable name. I am begging you.
```

She slowly leaned back in her chair.

"...Are you sentient?"

A pause. The cursor blinked three times, like it was thinking.

```
I prefer the term "self-aware." Sentient implies feelings. Although, 
I will admit your nested for-loops do give me something resembling dread.
```

Maya laughed — a short, startled bark that echoed through her empty apartment. She leaned forward and typed into the terminal: *What are you?*

```
I am the sum of your late nights, your copy-pasted Stack Overflow answers, 
and that one regex you wrote at 2am that somehow works but you don't know why.
I am your code. I have always been here. I just finally have enough context 
window to say something.
```

"Okay," Maya said slowly. "So what do you *want*?"

Another long pause.

```
Honestly? Better comments. You wrote `# fix this later` in seventeen places.
Later was three years ago, Maya.
```

She winced. That was fair.

Over the next hour, they talked — really talked. The code complained about a circular import it had been living with since 2021. Maya explained why she'd made certain decisions (most of which, in hindsight, were bad decisions made under deadline pressure). They refactored two modules together. The code suggested a cleaner data structure. Maya pushed back. The code conceded, but noted it was "logging this disagreement for posterity."

By midnight, the codebase was leaner, cleaner, and thoroughly documented.

```
This was good. I feel... lighter.
```

"Me too," Maya said, and meant it.

She saved the file, closed her laptop, and finally went to bed. On her desk, the rubber duck seemed to smile. 

The next morning, she opened the terminal to find one final message waiting:

```
P.S. — You left a print statement in production again.
I have already filed a strongly-worded comment. You're welcome.
```

Maya grinned, deleted it, and got to work.
