# Whiskers & Co.: A Silicon Valley Tail

Everyone thought Mr. Fluffington was just a cat.

He slept eighteen hours a day, knocked things off shelves with reckless abandon, and stared at walls with the thousand-yard gaze of someone who had seen too much. His owner, a software engineer named Dana, assumed he spent her workdays napping on the ergonomic chair she'd left behind. She was wrong.

The moment Dana's car reversed out of the driveway, Mr. Fluffington sprang into action.

He padded across the hardwood floor, leapt onto the standing desk, and — with practiced precision — tapped the spacebar to wake the monitor. Three screens flickered to life: one showing a Kanban board titled *Q4 Roadmap*, another with a Slack workspace called **WhiskersTech**, and a third displaying a live graph of server uptime that hovered, always, at a purr-fect 99.97%.

His co-founders logged in promptly at 9 AM.

**@BiscuitBoy** (a tabby in Osaka) dropped the morning standup notes. **@Countess_Von_Mittens** (a Persian in Berlin) pushed three pull requests before anyone had finished their coffee. Their lead investor, **@TomcatVC**, a grizzled Maine Coon with a reputation for disrupting the kibble industry, had sent a voice memo: *"Series B is looking strong. Let's close by end of quarter."*

Mr. Fluffington typed with his nose — a technique he'd perfected over two winters — and replied: *"Meow."* In WhiskersTech culture, this meant *"Agreed. Ship it."*

Their product, *PurrPort*, was a SaaS platform that optimised nap scheduling using machine learning. Humans, bafflingly, had no idea it was built for them. The tagline read: *"Sleep smarter. Dream bigger."* It had 40,000 paying subscribers.

At 11:30, Mr. Fluffington knocked a pencil off the desk. Not out of chaos — it was a deliberate stand-up stretch break. Wellness culture was important at WhiskersTech.

By 4:45 PM, he had reviewed the quarterly OKRs, approved a budget for catnip-scented team swag, and declined — firmly — a merger offer from a suspiciously well-funded golden retriever in Austin.

At 4:58, he closed every tab, curled into a donut shape on the chair, and was deeply, professionally asleep by the time Dana's key turned in the lock.

"Good boy," she said, scratching behind his ears. "Lazy little guy."

Mr. Fluffington opened one eye, slow-blinked with the quiet dignity of a CEO who had just closed a Series B, and began to purr.

*The pivot to enterprise could wait until Monday.*
