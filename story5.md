# The Buggy Chronicles of Dev404

Marcus had one rule about time travel: *never push to prod without testing first.*

He broke that rule constantly.

It started innocuously enough. He'd borrowed his colleague Priya's prototype temporal laptop — a sleek machine that smelled faintly of burnt silicon and paradox — just to "observe" the signing of the Magna Carta. But somewhere in the tall grass outside Runnymede, his elbow grazed the trackpad and he accidentally ran a script he'd been meaning to delete. The parchment King John signed? It now had a section titled `README.md` and a footer reading *"TODO: fix this before merge."*

Historians have been confused ever since.

Undeterred, Marcus tried again. He zipped back to 1969 to watch the Apollo 11 moon landing. He kept his hands firmly in his pockets this time. What he *didn't* account for was autocorrect. He'd been drafting a commit message — *"one small step for a man, one giant leap for refactoring"* — when his phone synced via some impossible Bluetooth vortex with Mission Control's telemetry system. Neil Armstrong's immortal words crackled across the radio:

*"That's one small step for a man, one giant leap for… null pointer exception."*

Houston did, in fact, have a problem.

By the time Marcus tumbled into Renaissance Florence, he'd developed what he called "temporal caution." He sat quietly at the back of Leonardo da Vinci's workshop, admiring the Mona Lisa taking shape on the easel. He opened his laptop — *just to take notes, he promised himself* — and tripped over a while loop he'd left running since the Magna Carta incident. The loop called a randomize function. The Mona Lisa's smile flickered between smirking, grinning, and one very unsettling emoji-style 😐 before Marcus furiously slammed his laptop shut.

She still looks ambiguous to this day. Marcus considers this one a draw.

His worst offense, however, was entirely accidental. Materializing in ancient Alexandria, he sneezed on the Library's main catalogue system — a meticulously organized set of scrolls — and knocked an entire shelf onto a torch. As the scrolls curled and the librarians gasped, his phone buzzed with a notification:

`⚠️ Deployment failed: rollback unavailable.`

Marcus sat down on a marble step, rubbed his face, and made a new rule: **no laptops in the timestream.** He'd use pencil and paper from now on.

He reached into his bag for a notepad.

His smartwatch pinged.

*Deploying patch to 44 BC…*

"*No, no, no—*"

Somewhere, Julius Caesar looked down at his toga and found a sticky note that read: *"Beware the merge conflict of March."*

Marcus was going to need a *lot* of rollback tokens.
